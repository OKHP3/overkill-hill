The First Diagram Is Usually a Liar

What happens when diagrams get cheap enough to be wrong on purpose? ROY (Return on Your Words) is the metric. A Council of AIs ran seven platforms against the same brief, plus one specialist purpose-built for Mermaid that should have dominated, and didn’t. v0.5 is now live: the Council scored each other’s diagrams using the same rubric the architect used, and every model was harder on itself than the architect was. Read the scores, the model interviews, and the meta-finding that changed how the Council was understood.

How OverKill Hill thinks

The “OverKill” in the name isn’t about complexity for its own sake. It’s about going far enough into the details that you trust the system when everything is moving fast.

Protocol-driven by default

We design AI interactions as protocols, not one-off prompts. Clear steps, defined states and escape hatches for when reality doesn’t match the diagram.

Performance forged, not presumed

The goal is to road-test systems with edge cases, weird inputs and real-world mess. If it breaks, we reforge it. We don’t blame the user.

Crafted to scale with humans

The goal is not “replace the human.” It’s to build tools that help ambitious people think more clearly, move faster and feel less alone in the complexity.
